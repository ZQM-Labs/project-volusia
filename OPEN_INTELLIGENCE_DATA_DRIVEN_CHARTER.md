1. WHAT WE MEAN BY OPEN-SOURCE INTELLIGENCE
============================================

1.1 OPEN SOURCE (NOT JUST FREE)
---------------------------------
Open-source intelligence in our context means:

   a) OPEN TOOLS
      We default to open-source software for infrastructure, analytics,
      and delivery. When we build something new and have no compelling
      reason to proprietary-lock it, we open it.

   b) OPEN DATA
      We publish public-interest data (economic indicators, tourism
      trends, infrastructure status, business formation stats) in
      machine-readable, license-free formats. If the data was created
      with public money or serves public good, it should be public.

   c) OPEN METHODOLOGY
      When we produce research, forecasts, or recommendations, we
      document the method so others can reproduce, challenge, or
      improve it. Secret methodology is a conflict of interest.

   d) OPEN ACCESS
      We do not gate insight behind paywalls, subscriptions, or
      membership tiers that exclude small businesses, residents, or
      community organizations. Basic intelligence is a public utility.

1.2 INTELLIGENCE (NOT JUST DATA)
---------------------------------
Raw data is not intelligence. Intelligence is:

   COLLECTED       — from reliable, documented sources
   CLEANED         — validated, deduplicated, standardized
   ANALYZED        — with stated methodology and known limitations
   DELIVERED       — to the decision-maker in time to matter
   UNDERSTOOD      — explained so a non-specialist can act on it

   We close the gap between "data exists" and "someone made a
   better decision because of it."

2. WHAT WE MEAN BY DATA-DRIVEN DECISION-MAKING
===============================================

2.1 THE STANDARD
-----------------
A decision is "data-driven" when:

   - The decision-maker can point to specific evidence that informed it
   - The evidence was gathered with a documented method
   - Alternative interpretations were considered
   - The expected outcome is stated in measurable terms before acting
   - The actual outcome is tracked and compared to the expectation
   - Lessons from the comparison feed back into future decisions

   If any of those steps is missing, the decision may be good — but
   it is not yet data-driven. We help our stakeholders climb that
   ladder.

2.2 THE LADDER OF DATA MATURITY
---------------------------------

   LEVEL 0 — GUT / HIERARCHY
      Decisions based on title, tradition, or gut feeling.
      Common. Not shameful. But improvable.

   LEVEL 1 — REPORTED
      Decisions informed by a report someone else produced.
      Problem: the decision-maker cannot verify the source.

   LEVEL 2 — ACCESSED
      The decision-maker can pull the underlying data themselves.
      Problem: they may lack the skill to interpret it.

   LEVEL 3 — ANALYZED
      The decision-maker (or a trusted analyst) can query, slice,
      and visualize the data. Patterns are visible.

   LEVEL 4 — MODELED
      The decision-maker can forecast scenarios: "If we do X,
      we expect Y, with Z% confidence."

   LEVEL 5 — INSTITUTIONALIZED
      The decision process is embedded in the organization.
      Decisions are logged, outcomes are tracked, models are
      updated. The organization learns.

   We meet stakeholders where they are on this ladder and help
   them climb — without pretending they're higher than they are.

3. WHO GETS ACCESS AND HOW
============================

3.1 BUSINESS OWNERS
--------------------
   Access to: local market benchmarks, customer demographics,
              industry trends, competitor landscape (public data),
              pricing intelligence, demand signals

   How:       Free dashboards, plain-language summaries,
              SMS alerts for key changes, quarterly market briefings

   Standard:  A food-truck owner should understand their market
              as well as a chain restaurant's VP of strategy.

3.2 RESIDENTS
--------------
   Access to: employment data, wage trends, cost-of-living metrics,
              service availability, transit performance, public
              spending (open budget), school performance, health
              outcomes by zip code

   How:       Public data portal, community meetings with data
              walkthroughs, translated summaries, mobile-first

   Standard:  A resident on a fixed income should be able to
              make data-informed decisions about where to live,
              work, and spend — not just react to headlines.

3.3 TOURISTS
-------------
   Access to: real-time conditions (surf, traffic, parking, weather),
              honest reviews (verified, not gamed), local business
              availability, event calendars, safety information

   How:       Public APIs, visitor-facing apps, digital kiosks,
              integration with mapping and booking platforms

   Standard:  A tourist should be able to plan and adjust their
              visit based on accurate, current intelligence — not
              on a blog post from three years ago.

3.4 INDUSTRY MOVERS
--------------------
   Access to: capital flow data, permitting velocity, infrastructure
              status, workforce availability, industry mix trends,
              demographic shifts, competitive landscape

   How:       Open APIs, quarterly economic reports, developer
              portal, investor data room (public layer)

   Standard:  An investor considering Volusia County should have
              the same quality of intelligence available as they
              would for Austin or Raleigh — no information penalty
              for choosing Volusia.

4. HOW WE OPERATIONALIZE THIS
===============================

4.1 BUILD, THEN OPEN
---------------------
We build internal capability first. Once a tool, dataset, or
methodology proves reliable inside Project Volusia, we evaluate
it for public release. If releasing it serves our mission and
does not compromise privacy or security, we open it.

4.2 OPEN BY DEFAULT, CLOSE BY EXCEPTION
-----------------------------------------
The question is not "Why should we release this?" The question is
"What specific harm would this release cause?" If the answer is
documented, material, and credible — we hold it. Otherwise, we open.

4.3 LICENSE TO ACT
-------------------
Data without the ability to act on it is frustration. We pair
every open dataset or tool with:

   - Documentation (what it is, what it is not)
   - Known limitations (where it is reliable, where it is thin)
   - A suggested use (what decision it can inform)
   - A feedback channel (report errors, request additions)

4.4 FEEDBACK LOOPS
-------------------
Every stakeholder who uses our intelligence should be able to tell
us: "This was helpful," "This was wrong," or "I needed something
you don't have." We track these signals as rigorously as we track
uptime. They are our quality metric.

4.5 PRIVACY AND ETHICS ARE NOT AFTERTHOUGHTS
----------------------------------------------
Open access does not mean open season on individuals. We:

   - Aggregate before releasing (no person-level public data)
   - Anonymize with verified techniques (not just name-stripping)
   - Audit for re-identification risk
   - Comply with HIPAA, FERPA, CCPA, and applicable Florida law
   - Maintain a public log of what we hold and why

   We do not maximize openness at the expense of privacy. We
   maximize BOTH through disciplined design.

5. MEASURING SUCCESS
=====================

   +---------------------------+-----------------------------------+
   | Metric                    | Target                            |
   +---------------------------+-----------------------------------+
   | Datasets published (open) | +12 per year                      |
   | API consumers (unique)    | +25% per year                     |
   | Business owner tool usage | +20% per year                     |
   | Resident portal visits    | +30% per year                     |
   | Tourist-facing API uptime | >= 99.9%                          |
   | Decision-maker satisfaction| >= 4.0/5.0 (quarterly survey)    |
   | Time-to-insight           | < 24 hours from question to answer|
   | Data quality complaints   | Tracked, resolved < 72 hours      |
   +---------------------------+-----------------------------------+

   VANITY METRICS WE DO NOT TRACK:
   - "Number of dashboards" (dashboards nobody open are waste)
   - "Records collected" (volume is not intelligence)
   - "AI/ML models deployed" (models nobody acts on are theater)

6. THE CHAIN OF LOGIC
=======================

   Open-source tools      → Lower cost of entry
   Open data              → Broader access to intelligence
   Open methodology       → Verifiable, trustworthy research
   Data-driven decisions  → Better outcomes for businesses,
                            residents, tourists, investors
   Better outcomes        → Stronger Volusia County economy
   Stronger economy       → More resources to reinvest in access
   More access            → The cycle continues

   This is the flywheel. We are building it intentionally, not
   hoping it emerges.

---

7. CLOSING COMMITMENT
======================

Project Volusia does not believe intelligence should be a luxury
good. We do not believe data-driven decision-making should be
limited to enterprises with data science teams. We do not believe
that living in Volusia County should mean accepting worse
information than you would have in a major tech hub.

We are here to close that gap — openly, measurably, and for everyone.

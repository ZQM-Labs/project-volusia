# STAKEHOLDER INTERVIEW GUIDE — PROJECT VOLUSIA
# Phase 1 Foundation Deliverable
# Date: 2026-09-03 | Version: 1.0

---

## 1. PURPOSE

This guide structures initial conversations with the four Project Volusia
constituencies. The goal is not statistical representativity in Phase 1 — it is
initial signal. We are testing the assumptions in our charter documents against
actual stakeholder voices.

Each interview takes 20-30 minutes. Themes (not quotes) are captured. Opinion is
distinguished from fact in the notes.

---

## 2. INTERVIEW PROTOCOL

### 2.1 BEFORE THE INTERVIEW
  - Confirm the stakeholder's group (business owner / resident / tourist proxy / industry mover)
  - Send a one-paragraph brief: who we are, what we are building, why their input matters
  - Prepare the relevant question set from section 3 below
  - Have a simple recording method (notes doc, not audio unless they consent)

### 2.2 OPENING (2 minutes)
  Introduce yourself. State the purpose:
    "We are building open data infrastructure for Volusia County — free tools
     and intelligence for businesses, residents, visitors, and investors. We are
     talking to people like you to make sure we build what actually matters,
     not just what we assume matters. This is not a sales conversation. There
     is no product to buy. Your honest input is the most useful thing."

  Confirm: "Is it OK if I take notes? I will capture themes, not quotes, and
  nothing attributed to you specifically."

### 2.3 CORE QUESTIONS (15-20 minutes)
  Use the relevant question set from section 3. Do not read them verbatim —
  use them as a guide to the conversation. Let the stakeholder go where they
  want to go. Capture what actually concerns them, not just answers to your
  questions.

### 2.4 CLOSING (3 minutes)
  - "Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?"
  - "If you could change one thing about how data and information flow in
     Volusia County, what would it be?"
  - "Can we follow up with you in a few months to show you what we built
     based on these conversations?"

### 2.5 AFTER THE INTERVIEW
  - Write up themes within 24 hours while memory is fresh
  - Log in the Stakeholder Input Summary spreadsheet/document
  - Flag any finding that challenges charter assumptions for the next sync

## 3. QUESTION SETS BY CONSTITUENCY

### 3.1 BUSINESS OWNERS

  CONTEXT QUESTIONS:
    - What kind of business do you run? (retail, hospitality, service, other)
    - How many employees?
    - How long have you been operating in Volusia County?

  DATA NEEDS:
    - When you make decisions about your business (hiring, pricing, expanding,
      hours, inventory), where do you get your information?
    - What is the hardest data to find when you need it?
    - Have you ever made a decision based on bad or stale data? What happened?
    - What would you want to know about your local market that you cannot
      easily find today?

  COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE:
    - How do you keep track of what other businesses in your area are doing?
    - What would knowing about local demand trends, customer demographics, or
      competitor landscape change about how you operate?

  BARRIERS:
    - What stops you from using more data in your business decisions?
      (cost, time, complexity, trust, access, skills)
    - What would a free, plain-language, local market dashboard need to
      contain for you to actually use it?

  TRUST:
    - When you see a statistic about Volusia County (tourism numbers, unemployment,
      growth), do you trust it? Why or why not?
    - What would make a data source trustworthy to you?

### 3.2 RESIDENTS

  CONTEXT QUESTIONS:
    - How long have you lived in Volusia County?
    - What part of the county? (beachside, DeLand, Deltona, New Smyrna, etc.)
    - What is your situation? (working, retired, fixed income, remote worker, etc.)

  DATA NEEDS:
    - When you need to make a decision about where to live, work, or spend money
      in Volusia, where do you look for information?
    - What is the most frustrating information to find?
    - Have you ever been surprised by something about the county (cost of living,
      school quality, crime, services) that you did not expect?

  PUBLIC SERVICES:
    - How do you find out about public services, transit, permitting, or
      community events?
    - What would make it easier to engage with local government?

  OPPORTUNITY:
    - What kind of work do you do? What kind of work would you like to do?
    - If you could see data about job trends, wage growth, or training
      opportunities in Volusia, would it change your plans?

  BARRIERS:
    - What stops you from using online tools and data more in your daily life?
    - What would make a public data portal actually useful to you?

### 3.3 TOURIST PROXIES (CVB, hospitality operators, visitor-facing service workers)

  CONTEXT QUESTIONS:
    - What is your role in the tourism ecosystem?
    - How long have you worked in Volusia tourism?

  DATA NEEDS:
    - What data do you currently use to understand tourism trends?
    - Where do you get visitor statistics, occupancy data, or event calendars?
    - What is the most important data point you cannot easily get?

  TOURIST EXPERIENCE:
    - What do tourists ask you about most? (things to do, where to eat,
      how to get around, safety, weather)
    - What information do tourists need before they arrive?
    - What information do they wish they had once they are here?

  GAPS:
    - What does Volusia County do well in communicating to visitors?
    - What does it do poorly?
    - If you could give every tourist one piece of accurate, real-time
      information, what would it be?

  SEASONALITY:
    - How does your information need change between peak season and off-season?
    - What would help you manage the seasonal swing better?

### 3.4 INDUSTRY MOVERS (investors, developers, officials, nonprofits, educators)

  CONTEXT QUESTIONS:
    - What is your role in the Volusia County economy?
    - How long have you been active in this space?

  DATA NEEDS:
    - When you evaluate Volusia County (for investment, development, policy,
      or program design), where do you get your data?
    - What is the most important data point that is hard to find or unreliable?
    - How does the quality of Volusia's data compare to other counties or
      metros you look at?

  COMPETITIVE POSITIONING:
    - What does Volusia County do well in presenting itself to people like you?
    - What does it do poorly?
    - What would make Volusia competitive with other mid-size counties for
      investment, talent, or development?

  BARRIERS:
    - What information gaps slow down your decisions about Volusia?
    - What would a high-quality, open data room for the county contain?

  PARTNERSHIP:
    - What would make you want to partner with or support an initiative like
      Project Volusia?
    - What would make you skeptical of it?

## 5. SAMPLING GUIDANCE FOR Q4 2026

  TARGET: 1-2 interviews per group in Q4 (8 total minimum)
  METHOD: Convenience sampling — people we can actually reach
  QUALITY BAR: Each interview produces at least one theme that either confirms
               or challenges a charter assumption

  PRIORITY ORDER:
    1. Business owners (highest leverage for Phase 2 dashboards)
    2. Industry movers (shape the data room and investor-facing materials)
    3. Residents (validate the public portal use case)
    4. Tourist proxies (harder to reach directly; use CVB and hospitality)

  DOCUMENT OUTPUT: STAKEHOLDER_INPUT_SUMMARY_Q4_2026.md in this folder

---

## 6. ETHICS AND PRIVACY

  - No recording without explicit consent
  - No personally identifiable information in summaries
  - Stakeholders can withdraw their input at any time
  - If a stakeholder says something that could harm them if attributed,
    leave it out entirely — the theme matters, not the source
  - Do not promise outcomes we cannot deliver
